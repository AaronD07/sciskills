Research Question:
Among borrowers in the South African consumer-credit field experiment, does receiving a longer suggested loan maturity lead to choosing a longer actual maturity and, in turn, taking a larger loan?

Hypothesis:
H0: Longer loan maturity does not increase loan size among borrowers in the study sample.
H1: Longer loan maturity increases loan size among borrowers in the study sample.

STUDY DESIGN:
- Type: Randomized field experiment embedded in consumer credit marketing
- Unit of observation: Individual borrower
- Source cohort: Former clients targeted with loan mailers in South Africa
- Assignment context: Mailers varied the example maturity shown and the interest rate offer
- Key analytic restriction: Focus on later-wave low- or medium-risk borrowers who took up a loan, were shown one maturity example, and were in the rate-reduction subsample

VARIABLE GLOSSARY:
- lnloansize: Log of the loan size taken up [OUTCOME]
- term: Loan maturity in months [TREATMENT] (endogenous)
- termshown6: Indicator that the mailer displayed a 6-month example maturity (instrument)
- termshown12: Indicator that the mailer displayed a 12-month example maturity (instrument)
- offer4: Offered interest rate control
- final4: Contract interest rate control
- yearlong: Indicator control for year-long offers
- low: Low-rate offer indicator control
- waved2: Experiment wave indicator control
- lnlastamount: Log of the borrower's previous loan amount (control)
- loansize: Loan size in levels
- branchuse: Branch identifier/control
- sales_grossincomecat2: Income-category control
